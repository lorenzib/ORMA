Hiking with your dog at altitude

The things that cause trouble on alpine trails are rarely the elevation. They are paws, heat, pace, and knowing when to turn back.

!

In an emergency

Act early

Call mountain rescue early rather than late. Know your dog's weight and any medication before you need to say them out loud, and know your nearest lift-assisted exit.

112Rescue, EU-wide 118Italy, medical
Heat

Heat & coats

  • A dense or double coat traps heat; heat load matters more than air temperature.
  • Start before 10am on warm days and turn back if panting stays heavy at rest.
  • Favour shaded, lakeside and forested routes when it is warm.
Terrain

Paws & terrain

  • Sun-baked rock and scree can burn pads; test with the back of your hand.
  • Sharp limestone and dense scrub nick thin skin, so check legs at breaks.
  • Long, steep descents stress large-breed hips and knees.
Water

Water & hydration

  • Carry more water than you think, plus a collapsible bowl.
  • Prefer routes with lake or stream access for cooling and drinking.
  • Avoid still, algae-green water; it can make dogs sick.
Hazards

Wildlife & livestock

  • Lead near grazing cattle and sheep; guardian dogs may confront loose dogs.
  • Give marmots, chamois and nesting birds a wide berth.
  • Keep dogs close through fenced pasture and close every gate.
Hazards

Cliffs & exposure

  • Keep dogs leashed on exposed ledges and near low or missing railings.
  • Watch for cornices and loose edges early season.
  • Recall is not enough near drop-offs; use the lead.
Access

Lifts & season

  • Many cable cars carry dogs, sometimes muzzled or ticketed; check first.
  • Shoulder-season trails can be icy at altitude even when valleys are green.
  • Note the last lift down if you are relying on one to exit.
