# Sources and editorial method

## Core veterinary references

- [ASPCA Toxic and Non-Toxic Plants](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants) — dog classifications, clinical-sign summaries, and the explicit warning that any plant material may cause vomiting or gastrointestinal upset.
- [ASPCA: Toxic and non-toxic plant list for dogs](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control/dogs-plant-list) — genus/species screening, including yarrow, buttercup, yew and Arum.
- [ASPCA: Rhododendron](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/rhododendron) — grayanotoxin risk and possible dog symptoms.
- [ASPCA: Western yew](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/western-yew) — Taxus toxic principles and possible dog symptoms; used with Merck's genus-level context for European yew.
- [ASPCA: Toxic and non-toxic flowers explained](https://www.aspca.org/news/do-you-know-which-flowers-and-plants-are-toxic-pets-our-experts-explain) — autumn crocus severity and non-toxic listings for roses and hens and chicks.
- [Merck Veterinary Manual: Plants poisonous to animals](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/special-pet-topics/poisoning/plants-poisonous-to-animals) — poisonous plant overview and autumn crocus effects.
- [Merck Veterinary Manual: Houseplants and ornamentals toxic to animals](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/toxicology/poisonous-plants/houseplants-and-ornamentals-toxic-to-animals) — grayanotoxins, cardiac glycosides, oxalate irritation and Taxus context.
- [VCA: Common emergencies in dogs](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/common-emergencies-in-dogs) — supports the instruction not to induce vomiting unless specifically directed by a veterinarian.

Each plant record contains the most relevant URLs in its `evidence` array. Sources were consulted on 2026-08-10.

## Editorial decisions

- Status describes the trail decision, not an exact toxic dose. Dose depends on plant, plant part, dog and circumstances.
- A green state is limited to normal proximity and sniffing. The copy never calls a wild plant edible.
- Red status is used where authoritative veterinary sources describe systemic, cardiac, organ, airway or rapidly progressive risk.
- Stinging nettle is retained as a useful trail-contact hazard but marked `confidence: limited` because the included core sources do not provide a species-specific dog toxicology monograph. It needs explicit veterinary review.
- Monkshood is marked medium confidence pending a dog-specific source/reviewer, while the emergency recommendation is intentionally conservative.
- Habitat, elevation and flowering months are broad editorial ranges for the European Alps, not live occurrence predictions. Climate, altitude, aspect and region shift flowering time.

## Required review before publication

1. A licensed veterinarian with poison-exposure experience should approve safety state, symptom wording and action copy.
2. A botanist familiar with the target Alpine regions should approve identification, lookalikes, habitat, elevation and season.
3. Legal/editorial review should approve any protected-species wording by country.
4. Recheck all links and add a named reviewer and review date. Schedule at least annual review and immediate review after a source changes.

## What this package intentionally does not include

- Diagnosis, toxic dose thresholds, first-aid treatment protocols or prognosis.
- A claim that every Alpine plant is covered.
- Photo-identification guarantees.
- Hard-coded emergency numbers for a multi-country Alpine audience.
- Unlicensed images or unsupported protected-status labels.
