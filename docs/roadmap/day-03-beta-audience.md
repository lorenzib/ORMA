# Day 3 — First beta audience

Status: complete

This day defines the eventual audience. It does not authorize or require
recruitment before the product is field-ready.

## Recommended initial region

Start in one compact Dolomites corridor, preferably Val Gardena and nearby
Alpe di Siusi trails.

This provides:

- a strong fit with the ORMA identity;
- varied low- and moderate-risk routes in a compact area;
- opportunities to test water, livestock, lift, and seasonal information;
- repeated access for internal validation;
- existing trail and guide content.

Cortina can follow. Savoy should be expanded after the complete workflow is
stable in one region.

If Val Gardena is not practical for repeated personal validation, the first
region should be the compact area the product owner can visit and verify most
reliably.

## Primary beta user

> A dog owner who hikes occasionally, uses a phone for navigation, and wants
> more confidence about whether a route suits their dog.

The participant:

- has completed walks with their dog;
- understands their dog's normal fitness;
- plans routes personally;
- can follow an offline-download flow;
- is willing to provide structured feedback;
- does not need to be an expert hiker or technical user.

## Dog and device diversity

The cohort should include:

- small, medium, and large dogs;
- a senior dog and a young energetic dog;
- a heat-sensitive dog or relevant health profile;
- mixed or unknown breeds;
- both low-fitness and trail-experienced dogs;
- iPhone/Safari and Android/Chrome users;
- older or lower-powered phones;
- participants with varied technical confidence.

## Beta waves

### Wave 0 — Controlled internal field test

Three to five trusted people test only familiar, low-risk routes.

Focus:

- download completion;
- airplane-mode reopening;
- GPS acquisition;
- refresh and browser-close restoration;
- battery behavior;
- end-hike flow.

Nobody should deliberately leave the route or choose exposed terrain to test a
warning.

### Wave 1 — Guided beta

Twelve to fifteen target users:

1. create a dog profile;
2. select a suitable route;
3. explain the recommendation;
4. download the route;
5. test it in airplane mode before leaving;
6. use it during a normal hike;
7. provide structured post-hike feedback.

### Wave 2 — Wider regional beta

Thirty to fifty users only after Wave 1 blockers are resolved. This wave
expands device coverage, dog profiles, connectivity conditions, community
moderation, and unobserved behavior.

## Recruitment timing

Recruitment is intentionally deferred until the later beta-preparation phase.
The first public cohort should not be recruited until:

- offline downloads work;
- airplane-mode reopening works;
- an active hike survives refresh and browser closure;
- GPS and off-route behavior have been tested internally;
- recommendations are consistent and explainable;
- security and moderation rules exist;
- high-priority accessibility and usability problems are resolved;
- the product owner has completed the full journey on a safe route.

Before then, one to three trusted people may perform low-risk indoor usability
checks, such as selecting a trail or reopening it in airplane mode.

## Recruitment channels for later

Potential channels:

- local dog-walking and hiking groups;
- dog trainers and canine educators;
- independent veterinary practices;
- dog-friendly accommodation hosts;
- rifugi near easy routes;
- local tourism communities;
- regional Facebook, Reddit, and hiking communities;
- personal contacts who own dogs but have not seen ORMA.

## Participant safety rules

Participants must:

- choose a familiar, low-risk trail;
- avoid using ORMA as their sole emergency preparation;
- download and verify the route before leaving;
- carry sufficient battery and water;
- follow closures and local instructions;
- never deliberately go off-route to test warnings;
- stop using the beta if it distracts from safe hiking;
- report misleading safety information immediately.

## Wave 1 success thresholds

- At least 80% download without assistance.
- At least 90% reopen the trail in airplane mode.
- At least 90% acquire a usable GPS position.
- No participant loses the route after refresh or browser closure.
- At least 80% correctly explain the recommendation.
- At least 80% say the selected trail suited their dog.
- Every misleading safety claim is investigated.
- No critical security or navigation failure remains unresolved.

## Agreed future audience

> Twelve to fifteen occasional dog hikers in one compact Dolomites region,
> representing varied dogs, devices, and technical confidence, testing
> familiar low-risk routes through structured observation and post-hike
> feedback.
