# Day 1 — Product promise

Status: complete

## Primary user

A dog owner planning a hike in the Dolomites who wants to know whether a
particular trail is appropriate for their dog, not merely whether dogs are
legally permitted.

The initial user:

- plans primarily on a phone;
- knows their dog's general fitness and limitations;
- may not know the local trail network;
- wants a clear, evidence-backed recommendation;
- cares about heat, water, terrain, exposure, restrictions, and offline access.

## Primary problem

General hiking products describe trails for humans. Dog owners must combine
maps, forecasts, local regulations, reviews, and tourism sites, yet may still
not know:

- whether the terrain is manageable for their dog;
- whether sufficient water and shade are available;
- whether exposed or paw-unfriendly sections exist;
- whether livestock, lifts, or restrictions create problems;
- how current or trustworthy the information is;
- whether the route will remain available when mobile coverage disappears.

## Product promise

> ORMA helps you choose a trail that suits your dog, carry the route
> offline, and understand where you are while hiking.

Supporting message:

> Before leaving, check dog-specific risks and download the trail. On the walk,
> follow your position using GPS even when mobile coverage disappears.

Short user-facing message:

> Find walks that fit your dog, not just your destination.

## What ORMA promises

ORMA will:

- evaluate trails from a dog-specific perspective;
- explain why a trail appears suitable or unsuitable;
- separate verified facts, community reports, and automated estimates;
- show when important information is missing or outdated;
- let account holders prepare essential trail information for offline use;
- show the user's position relative to the downloaded route;
- provide actionable warnings without presenting uncertainty as safety.

## What ORMA does not promise

ORMA does not promise that:

- a trail is completely safe;
- conditions have not changed since the latest report;
- a recommendation replaces veterinary, emergency, or local professional advice;
- every dog with a similar profile will respond identically;
- imported routes have been personally inspected;
- water, weather, lifts, access rules, or GPS accuracy will remain unchanged;
- a high match score removes the owner's responsibility to observe their dog;
- ORMA is an emergency-rescue or certified turn-by-turn navigation service.

## Product principles

1. Explain, do not merely score.
2. Unknown is different from safe.
3. Dog-specific information comes before generic trail statistics.
4. Evidence and freshness must be visible.
5. Safety warnings must be actionable.
6. One excellent region is more valuable than many unreliable regions.
7. Community reports supplement verified information; they do not silently
   rewrite it.
8. "Ready offline" is shown only after every essential resource has been stored
   and verified.

## North-star outcome

> The percentage of users who choose an appropriate trail, successfully prepare
> it for offline use, complete the hike with ORMA, and confirm that the
> trail suited their dog.

A practical beta proxy is:

```text
completed hikes rated appropriate
÷
completed hikes with a suitability response
```

## Core product identity

ORMA is both:

1. a dog-specific trail decision tool; and
2. an offline trail companion.
