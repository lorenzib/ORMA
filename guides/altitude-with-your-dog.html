Safety library / Altitude & exertion

Your dog at altitude

Altitude, effort and heat can look alike. Know your dog’s normal and act early when it changes.

2 min guide

In 20 seconds

  • Ask your vet first if your dog has heart or lung problems.
  • Turn back for a new cough, low energy or odd breathing.
  • Keep the first day short and easy.
  • Never give human altitude or pain medication.
Less oxygen

Why it’s harder up high

Higher up, there’s less oxygen. A fast climb plus exercise works the heart and lungs harder and can bring out a heart or breathing problem you hadn’t noticed at home. Stop for a new cough, low energy or odd breathing.

Ask your vet first
Book a check before the trip if your dog has heart or lung disease, coughs, faints, tires easily or struggled at altitude before. Puppies, seniors and flat-faced dogs need extra care.

No human altitude medication
Never give human altitude or pain medication unless your vet prescribes it for your dog. Don’t use medicine to push past warning signs.

Arriving

Keep the first day easy

Even fit dogs can struggle after a fast drive or lift up.

Before you go up

  1. Learn their normal

    Notice their breathing, walk, appetite and energy at home.

  2. Plan an easy way back

    Pick a short route with an early turnaround and an easy way down.

Once you arrive

  1. Settle in first

    Give water, take a calm walk and skip the big route on arrival day.

  2. Watch their recovery

    Rest early. Turn back if breathing, walking or behaviour doesn’t settle.
