Meeting a livestock guardian dog with your own dog

See the herd early, leash your dog and detour. Release the lead only if actual dog-to-dog conflict begins.

2 min guide
Understand the job

The dog is protecting the flock

Barking, approaching or placing itself between you and livestock are deterrent behaviours. They are not a reason to approach, test the dog or continue through the herd.

Default move: leash early and go around

Your dog may be read as a potential predator. A wide, calm detour before either dog reacts is safer than managing contact.

Use the moment

Three moves, in order

Prevent contact first. Release the lead only if actual dog-to-dog conflict begins.

01
You see the herd first

Leash and detour

Keep your dog close and go widely around the flock, never between animals. Turn back if there is no safe gap.

02
A guardian approaches

Stop and become uninteresting

Stay calm and still, or move very slowly. Avoid staring; keep poles down and let the dog assess you from a distance.

03
Actual dog-to-dog conflict

Release the lead

Leash is the default while distance is still possible. If conflict actually begins, French national guidance says to release the lead. Never pick your dog up; move away calmly and turn back.

Never escalate the guardian-dog encounter

Do not approach the flock, pet or feed the guardian, throw objects or place yourself closer for a photograph.

No runningNo shoutingNo staringNo raised polesNo petting
Before the trail

Make room to avoid the encounter

  • Check local signs, current pastoral maps and recent trail reports.
  • Avoid the pasture if dogs are prohibited or no wide detour exists.
  • Leash at the first clue: bells, a cattle grid, livestock or a warning sign.

Cattle are different: give them—especially cows with calves—a wide berth. Keep your dog leashed, but release the lead if cattle chase you so you can reach safety separately.

References checked 25 August 2026: French National Parks guardian-dog guidance and its official DREAL leaflet, which says to keep a companion dog leashed but release the lead if actual conflict begins; Vallée d’Aulps current guidance; and UK Department for Environment, Food & Rural Affairs cattle advice, which separately advises releasing the lead if cattle chase you. Local signs and restrictions override general guidance. Animal behaviour is never guaranteed.
