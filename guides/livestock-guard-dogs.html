Meeting a livestock guardian dog with your own dog

Spot guarded flocks early, go wide around them, and know what to do if a guardian dog comes over.

2 min guide

In 20 seconds

  • Put your dog on a short lead at the first sign of a flock: bells, a cattle grid, animals or a warning sign.
  • Go wide around the flock, never through it. Turn back if there is no safe way round.
  • If a guardian dog comes over, stop, stay calm and don’t stare. Then back away slowly.
Spot them early

How to recognise guardian dogs

Guardian dogs live with the flock and protect it on their own, day and night. Barking, running towards you or blocking the path is a warning to keep away.

  • Large, often white dogs, such as the Maremmano-Abruzzese or the Pyrenean mountain dog.
  • Hard to see: they often lie among the sheep or goats, so you may only notice them when you are close.
  • More alert at dusk and when the flock is moving.
  • Signs at gates and fences warn that guardian dogs are working there. Swiss signs ask you to keep your distance and advise against bringing a pet dog.
Before the pasture

Before you walk into a pasture

Italian and Swiss guidance advises against bringing a pet dog where guardian dogs work. If your walk takes you there anyway:

  • Check local signs, pasture maps and recent trail reports before you set off.
  • Put your dog on a short lead at the first clue: bells, a cattle grid, animals or a sign. Don’t pick it up.
  • Go wide around the flock, never through it or between the animals. On a bike, get off and push.
  • Turn back if dogs are banned there or there is no wide way round.

Cattle are different: give them, especially cows with calves, a wide berth. Keep your dog on the lead, but let go if cattle chase you, so you can both reach safety separately.

On the spot

If a guardian dog comes over

The dog wants to check that you are no threat. Give it time and don’t try to get past.

  1. 1

    Stop and stay calm

    Stand still, or move very slowly. Don’t give the guardian dog commands: it won’t obey them.

  2. 2

    Don’t stare

    Keep facing the dog but avoid eye contact. Keep your poles down.

  3. 3

    Keep your dog close

    Hold it on a short lead at your side. Don’t pick it up.

  4. 4

    Back away slowly

    Step back without turning your back until the dog stops barking. Then go wide around the flock, or turn back.

Never run, shout, throw stones or wave sticks. Don’t pet or feed the guardian dog, or go closer for a photo.

If a fight starts, let go of the lead

The lead is right while you can still keep your distance. If the guardian dog actually attacks your dog, French national guidance says to let go of the lead. Never pick your dog up. Move away calmly and turn back.

Afterwards

After an incident

Once you are well away from the flock:

  • A bite to a person needs first aid and a doctor.
  • A bite to your dog needs a vet, even if the wound looks small.
  • Report it to the shepherd or farm and to the local police or commune, with the place and time. In Switzerland, the cantonal veterinary office investigates guardian-dog bites.
