Meeting a livestock guardian dog with your own dog

Your safest encounter is the one you prevent: notice the herd early, leash before the pasture and create distance around it.

3 min guideLast reviewed 25 August 2026
Understand the job

The dog is protecting the flock

Barking, approaching or placing itself between you and livestock are deterrent behaviours. They are not a reason to approach, test the dog or continue through the herd.

Default move: leash early and go around

Your dog may be read as a potential predator. A wide, calm detour before either dog reacts is safer than managing contact.

Use the card that matches the moment

Three decisions

Move from prevention to retreat; do not try to “win” the encounter.

You see the herd first

Detour before contact

  • Leash your dog and keep them close.
  • Go widely around the flock, not between animals.
  • If there is no safe space, turn back.
A guardian approaches

Stop and become uninteresting

  • Stay calm; stop or move very slowly.
  • Do not stare, shout, run or raise poles.
  • Speak quietly and let the dog assess from a distance.
Direct dog-to-dog contact

Do not trap your dog at your legs

Keep the leash as the default while you can still create distance. If direct contact becomes unavoidable, Explore Savoie advises releasing your dog so they can communicate and retreat. Move away calmly and turn back if the guardian does not settle.

Never escalate the guardian-dog encounter

Do not approach the flock, pet or feed the guardian, throw objects or place yourself closer for a photograph.

No runningNo shoutingNo staringNo raised polesNo petting
Before the trail

Plan for the pasture

Prefer visible crossings

Short pasture sections with room to detour give you time to see livestock and working dogs before they react.

Read recent reports

Herds move. Check current trail reports and follow local signs even when an older report says the pasture was clear.

Leash at the first clue

Clip on before the cattle grid, bells or animals—not after a guardian dog appears.

Cattle are a separate hazard

Give cattle—especially cows with calves—a wide berth and keep your dog leashed. If cattle chase or threaten you, official countryside guidance advises releasing the dog so you can reach safety separately. Follow local signs and avoid the field when a safer alternative exists.
