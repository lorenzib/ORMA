Livestock guardian dogs

Spot a guarded flock early, go wide, and know what to do if a guardian dog comes over.

3 min guide

In 20 seconds

  • At the first sign of a flock (bells, a cattle grid, animals or a sign), put your dog on a short lead.
  • Go wide around the flock, never through it, or turn back.
  • If a guardian dog comes over, stop, stay calm and don’t stare. Then back away slowly.
On the trail

What you might see

Italian and Swiss guidance says not to bring a pet dog where guardian dogs work. Before you go, check local signs, pasture maps and recent trail reports.

Signs of a flock

Bells, a cattle grid or a sign

Put your dog on a short lead and go wide around the animals, never through them. On a bike, get off and push. Swiss signs ask you to keep your distance.

Guardian dog in sight

A large white dog among the sheep

Often a Maremmano-Abruzzese or Pyrenean mountain dog. It can lie hidden in the flock and is more alert at dusk and when the flock moves. Don’t pick your dog up. If dogs are banned or you can’t go wide, turn back.

It comes over

Barking, running at you or blocking the path

It wants you away from its flock. Follow the three steps below.

Cattle are different. Keep well away, especially from cows with calves. Keep your dog on the lead, but let go if cattle chase you, so you can each get away.

If it comes over

Stop, then back away

It wants to check you’re not a threat, so give it time and don’t try to get past. Never run, shout, throw stones or wave sticks. Don’t pet or feed it.

Stop and stay calmStand still or move very slowly. It won’t obey your commands, so don’t give any.
Don’t stareFace it, but don’t look it in the eye. Poles down, and your dog close on a short lead. Don’t pick your dog up.
Back away slowlyStep back without turning round until it stops barking. Then go wide or turn back.
If a fight starts, let go of the lead

Keep the lead on while you can keep your distance. If the guardian dog actually attacks your dog, French national guidance says to let go of the lead. Never pick your dog up. Move away calmly and turn back.

Afterwards

After an incident

Once you’re well away:

See a doctorIf a person is bitten, give first aid and see a doctor.
See a vetIf your dog is bitten, see a vet, even for a small wound.
Report itTell the shepherd or farm and the local police or commune where and when it happened. In Switzerland, the cantonal veterinary office investigates.
