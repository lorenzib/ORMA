Dog rules in the Dolomites

Leads, muzzles, park rules and bags: what applies where.

3 min guide

In 20 seconds

  • Carry a lead no longer than 1.5 m and a muzzle. Buses, trains and cable cars usually want both on.
  • In nature parks, keep your dog on the lead. In the Dolomiti Bellunesi National Park, dogs can’t enter zone A at all.
  • Pick up after your dog, and keep it on a short lead near grazing cattle.
Everywhere in Italy

Three things to carry

A national ordinance sets the basics. It was renewed in 2026 and runs to September 2027. You are responsible for anything your dog does.

Lead

No longer than 1.5 m

In towns and any place open to the public, except fenced dog areas.

Muzzle

Always in your pack

Put it on if there is a risk to people or animals, or if an official asks. Soft or basket muzzles both count.

Bags

Pick it up

In towns you must pick up after your dog and carry bags to do it. On the trail, carry it out too.

Papers. Dogs living in Italy must be microchipped and registered. Visiting dogs travel on the EU pet passport, and trains in Trentino ask to see it.

Nature parks

Where the rules are stricter

Most Dolomites parks welcome dogs on a lead. One keeps them out of its strictest zones.

  • Dolomiti Bellunesi National ParkDogs on a lead, and no dogs in zone A, not even on marked paths that cross it. Check the park map before you go.
  • South Tyrol nature parksSchlern-Rosengarten, Puez-Geisler, Fanes-Sennes-Prags, Drei Zinnen and the others: close supervision, a lead, and no dogs roaming free.
  • Adamello-BrentaLead at all times.
  • Dolomiti d’AmpezzoLead at all times. Loose dogs disturb and can injure wildlife.
  • Dolomiti FriulaneLead in the nature reserve zones.
  • Paneveggio–Pale di San MartinoNever leave your dog unattended. We couldn’t confirm a written lead rule, so follow the signs at the park entrance.
South Tyrol

A few extra rules

The province adds to the national rules.

Off the lead means a muzzleIn streets, squares, public parks and buildings, a dog off the lead must wear a muzzle. Dogs may never roam.
Short lead on the almNear grazing cattle, keep your dog on a short lead and stay well away from cows with calves.
DNA for resident dogsDogs that live in South Tyrol must have a DNA profile on file. A bill to scrap the rule was filed in October 2026.
Getting there

Buses, trains and cable cars

Small pets in a bag or carrier usually travel free. Bigger dogs need a lead, often a muzzle, and sometimes a ticket. See Cable cars with your dog for getting a nervous dog used to the muzzle.

  • South Tyrol buses and trainsLead for the whole trip, muzzle as a rule, no dogs on seats. €2 a trip or €3.50 a day.
  • Trentino TrasportiBuses, trains and cable cars: a muzzle and a lead of up to 1.5 m for every dog, whatever its size. Larger dogs need a ticket.
  • Dolomiti Bus (Belluno)Larger dogs pay a ticket and need a lead and muzzle. The driver can refuse animals when the bus is full.
  • Cable carsExpect a lead and a muzzle. Some charge for dogs: the Seiser Alm cable car is €5 one way.
Local signs win

A comune or park can be stricter than this guide, and rules change. If a sign at the trailhead says lead or no dogs, follow it. In Friuli Venezia Giulia, for example, a loose dog in a park that requires a lead can mean a fine of €25 to €250.
