Which Alpine plants should your dog avoid?

Use the cards to see what your dog should avoid and which changes to monitor. The guide covers open trail, woodland, wet ground and the village edges around an Alpine trip.

Think your dog ate an unknown plant?

Move away, take clear photos and call a veterinarian or emergency clinic promptly. Do not induce vomiting or give food, milk, charcoal or medication unless a veterinary professional tells you to.

What to photograph and note
  1. The whole plant, leaves, flowers or fruit and where it was growing.
  2. The time, likely plant part and possible amount.
  3. A sample only if it is safe and legal to collect; use gloves for unknown plants.

About the cards and photographs

This 33-record awareness catalogue is not an identification guarantee or an exhaustive local flora: appearance changes through the season, and similar species can carry different risks.

All card photographs are reusable Commons references with creator and licence details behind the © control. The catalogue is not presented as approved by a veterinarian or regional botanist.

Plant-specific evidence was checked on 26 August 2026. Core references: ASPCA Animal Poison Control and the Merck Veterinary Manual. Leave wild plants in place and use the cards’ avoid and monitor notes for trail decisions.

This is general information, not a diagnosis or a substitute for veterinary care.
