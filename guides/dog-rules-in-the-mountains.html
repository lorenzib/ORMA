Dog rules in the mountains

Leads, muzzles, parks and pastures: what applies almost everywhere.

3 min guide

In 20 seconds

  • Carry a lead, a muzzle and bags. Buses, trains and cable cars usually want the lead and muzzle on.
  • In parks, reserves and pastures, keep your dog on the lead. Some national parks ban dogs from their core, even on a lead.
  • Rules change from place to place. The sign at the start of the walk wins.
Almost everywhere

Three things to carry

Each country and region adds its own rules, but these come up almost everywhere in the Alps. You are responsible for anything your dog does.

Lead

Short, and always with you

Use it in towns, on busy paths and wherever a sign asks. In Italy it may be no longer than 1.5 m.

Muzzle

In your pack

Buses, trains and cable cars often ask for one. In Italy you must always carry one. Soft or basket muzzles both count.

Bags

Pick it up

In towns you must pick up after your dog. On the trail, carry it out too.

Papers. Your dog needs a microchip. To cross a border it needs an EU pet passport with a valid rabies vaccine. Switzerland accepts it too.

Where rules are stricter

Put the lead on here

These places have stricter rules in most of the Alps.

  • National park coresSome ban dogs completely, even on a lead and on marked paths. Check the park map before you go.
  • Nature parks and reservesMost welcome dogs on a lead. A few reserves ban them, so read the board at the entrance.
  • Pastures with cattle or sheepShort lead, keep your distance and go round the herd. If cows charge, let go of the lead. See Livestock guard dogs for flocks with guard dogs.
  • Spring and early summerWild animals have their young. Some areas ask for a lead in woods and meadows from April to July. See Wildlife on the trail.
Getting there

Buses, trains and cable cars

See Cable cars with your dog for getting a nervous dog used to the muzzle.

  • Small dogsIn a bag or carrier, they usually travel free.
  • Buses and trainsLead for the whole trip, often a muzzle, no dogs on seats. Bigger dogs usually need a ticket.
  • Cable carsExpect a lead and a muzzle. Some charge for dogs.
Before you go

How to check the local rule

A commune or park can be stricter than this guide, and rules change.

Read the walk pageWe note the park or commune rule when we find one.
Ask the tourist officeIt knows the local rules, including summer bans on some pastures.
Follow the signIf a sign at the start says lead or no dogs, follow it.
By country

What is different

The main rules in the countries ORMA covers.

  • ItalyA lead no longer than 1.5 m in towns and public places, and a muzzle always in your pack. In South Tyrol, a dog off the lead in town must wear a muzzle. No dogs in zone A of the Dolomiti Bellunesi National Park.
  • SwitzerlandEach canton and commune sets its own rules. The Swiss National Park bans dogs, even on a lead. Federal hunting reserves ask for a lead.
  • FranceNo dogs in the core of national parks such as Vanoise and Écrins, even on a lead, and in some nature reserves. Some communes ban dogs on alpine pastures in summer.
