Winter walks with your dog

Snow makes the Dolomites quiet and beautiful. Pick a cleared path, watch for cold, and be back before dark.

2 min guide

In 20 seconds

  • Walk on cleared winter paths. Keep off ski runs, sled runs and cross-country tracks.
  • Be off the trail by about 4 pm in December and January.
  • After salted streets, rinse and dry your dog’s paws. Clear snow from between the toes.

Winter walks are shorter and slower. Paths are packed snow or ice, the sun goes early, and your dog gets cold faster when you stop.

Plan ahead

Where to walk

Cleared winter paths. Tourist boards clear or roll some paths every winter. These are the walks to choose. Our Winter walks with your dog collection lists ones we have checked.

Not on ski runs. Italian law does not allow walking on ski runs except in an emergency. Cross only at marked points, quickly, with your dog on a short lead.

Not on cross-country tracks. Many winter paths run right next to them. The Seiser Alm board, for one, asks walkers and dogs to keep off. Paw prints ruin the tracks.

Sled runs. Avoid them. If a path is shared with sledges, walk at the edge in single file with your dog on a short lead.

Read your dog

Cold

Who feels it first: small dogs, short or thin coats, puppies, older dogs and dogs with joint pain or illness. A dog coat helps, above all when you stop for a break.

Signs, and what to doFrom shivering to hypothermia
StageWhat you see
ColdShivering, lifting paws, slowing down, whining, wanting to turn back.
Too coldDrowsy or confused, clumsy, pale gums. Shivering can stop when a dog is very cold.

Get your dog dry and out of the wind, wrap them in a coat or blanket and warm them slowly. Call a vet. Don’t wait and see.

Paws

Salt, ice and snowballs

Salt on village streets and car parks dries and cracks pads. Rinse and dry paws, legs and belly after the walk, and don’t let your dog eat salty snow.

Snowballs build up in the fur between the toes and hurt. Trim that fur short, and clear the snow gently as you go. Boots or paw balm help on salt and crusted ice.

Antifreeze in car parks tastes sweet and is very poisonous. If your dog licks some, go to a vet straight away. More on paws in Paw protection.

On the day

Short days, ice and lifts

Daylight is short. In mid-December the sun sets around 4:30 pm in Bolzano and Cortina, and earlier in deep valleys. Start by late morning and be back by about 4 pm. A light on the collar helps on the way to the car.

Frozen lakes and streams. Keep your dog off the ice and on a lead near the shore. If your dog goes through the ice, don’t go after them: call 112.

Water. Streams and fountains freeze, and snow is not a drink. Carry water in an insulated bottle.

Rules still apply. In Italy dogs are on a lead of up to 1.5 m in public places, with a muzzle to hand. Local dog rules apply in winter too. In South Tyrol the hunting season runs to mid-December, and dogs must never roam free outside villages.

Lifts. Most Dolomites lifts take dogs on a lead and in a muzzle, and some charge a small fee. Ask at the lift. See Cable cars with your dog.

Avalanches. Our winter walks stay on valley paths away from slopes. If you go higher, read the avalanche bulletin first: avalanche.report for South Tyrol and Trentino, ARPAV for the Belluno Dolomites. Off cleared paths in avalanche terrain, the law asks you to carry a transceiver, shovel and probe.
