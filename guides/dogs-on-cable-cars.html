Cable cars with your dog

Prepare calmly, recognise fear or nausea early, and know when not boarding is the healthier choice.

3 min guideReviewed August 2026
Start with your dog

Is today a gondola day?

A lift can remove a difficult climb, but the cabin itself must still feel manageable.

Likely readySettles in small spaces and recovers easily from noise.
Plan carefullyYoung, older, motion-sick or unsure around crowds and doors.
Choose another routePanics, struggles to breathe or must be forced inside.
!
Never force a frightened dog into the cabin.Dragging, carrying or waiting for panic to pass can deepen the fear. Step away and use the alternative plan.
Bring and check
  • Practised basket muzzleRoom to pant and drink.
  • Secure harness and short leashNo retractable line in the queue.
  • Water and a plan BA lower route if boarding is not right today.
  • Current operator rulesRequirements differ by lift and season.
Before lift day

Prepare in three small steps

1

Practise the muzzle

Build comfort at home with rewards. Keep every practice short enough that your dog can still relax, eat and choose to move away. The station queue should never be the first fitting.

2

Visit without boarding

Watch the cabins from a comfortable distance, then leave before worry begins.

3

Take the easiest ride

Choose a quiet time, a short journey and the least crowded cabin available.

Check before travelling

Rules in brief

Policies can change. Make sure to check lifts opening ahead of your hiking day!

Cortina SkiworldListed lifts welcome dogs without a fare; leash and muzzle required.
LagazuoiLeash and muzzle required; larger dogs need a dedicated ticket.
Val GardenaTicket, leash and muzzle required; dogs may not occupy seats.
Chamonix valleyMany lifts accept dogs, with important lift and nature-reserve exceptions.
A healthy choice

Not boarding is a valid plan

A gondola is a convenience, not a test. If your dog cannot recover at the station, choose a lower walk, a quieter day or a route without a lift.
