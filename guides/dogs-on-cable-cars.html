Safety library / Access & transport

Cable cars with your dog

Prepare calmly, watch for fear or nausea, and know when not to board.

2 min guide

In 20 seconds

  • Never force a frightened dog into a cabin. Take another route.
  • Practise the muzzle at home with treats, well before the trip.
  • Check the lift’s dog rules. Many ask for a lead and a muzzle.
Your dog

Is today a gondola day?

A lift saves a climb, but your dog still has to cope with the cabin.

Likely readySettles in small spaces and shrugs off noise.
Plan carefullyYoung, older, motion sick or nervous around crowds and doors.
Take another routePanics, struggles to breathe or has to be forced in, or the route needs an open chairlift.
Never force a frightened dog into the cabin.Dragging, carrying or waiting out the panic can make the fear worse. Step away and use your other plan.
At home

Make the muzzle mean treats

Use a basket muzzle your dog can pant and drink in. Let them choose each step, and never push it onto their face.

  1. Treat through the muzzleHold it still with a treat inside and let your dog put their nose in. Remove it before they pull back.
  2. Repeat without fasteningDo several calm nose-ins over short sessions.
  3. Fasten from behindOnce that’s easy, fasten the strap behind the ears for a second or two. Treat through the front, then take it off.
  4. Add time slowlyAdd a few seconds at a time while your dog stays relaxed, then practise walking, drinking and eating treats in it.

If your dog freezes, paws at it or stops taking food, go back to an easier step next time.

Getting ready

Three steps before lift day

1

Rehearse at home

Practise the whole routine at home. The station queue shouldn’t be the first full fitting.

2

Visit without boarding

Watch the cabins from a comfortable distance and leave before your dog starts to worry.

3

Take the easiest ride

Pick a quiet time, a short ride and the emptiest cabin.

Open chairlifts are different: an open seat a dog can fall from. ORMA marks routes that need one as not recommended, unless your dog weighs 8 kg or less and rides them calmly on your lap.
