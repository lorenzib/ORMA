Health & access

Cable cars with your dog

Prepare calmly, recognise fear or nausea early, and know when not boarding is the healthier choice.

3 min guide
Start with your dog

Is today a gondola day?

A lift can remove a difficult climb, but the cabin itself must still feel manageable.

Likely readySettles in small spaces and recovers easily from noise.
Plan carefullyYoung, older, motion-sick or unsure around crowds and doors.
Choose another routePanics, struggles to breathe or must be forced inside.
Never force a frightened dog into the cabin.Dragging, carrying or waiting for panic to pass can deepen the fear. Step away and use the alternative plan.
Practise at home

Make the muzzle predict treats

Use a basket muzzle with room to pant and drink. Let your dog choose each step; do not push the muzzle onto the face.

  1. Treat through the muzzleHold it still, place a treat inside and let your dog put their nose in voluntarily. Remove it before they pull away.
  2. Repeat without fasteningBuild several calm nose-in repetitions over short sessions.
  3. Fasten from behindWhen your dog is comfortable, bring the strap behind the ears, fasten for one or two seconds, feed a treat through the front, then remove it.
  4. Add time graduallyIncrease by a few seconds while your dog stays relaxed. Practise walking, drinking and taking treats before lift day.

If your dog freezes, paws at the muzzle or stops taking food, pause and return to an easier step next time.

Before lift day

Prepare in three small steps

1

Rehearse the full routine

Practise fastening, treating, walking and drinking at home. The station queue should never be the first full fitting.

2

Visit without boarding

Watch the cabins from a comfortable distance, then leave before worry begins.

3

Take the easiest ride

Choose a quiet time, a short journey and the least crowded cabin available.
