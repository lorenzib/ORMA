Guide · Paws & terrain

Rock changes the walk.

Pads that cope at home can wear through in a day. The safest plan begins with the surface underfoot, then follows a simple routine: prepare, check and respond.

Read the surface.

Texture matters more than temperature alone. Look at what your dog will repeat for hours, not only the hardest-looking section.

Before the big day

Train for the trail, not just the distance.

Routine walks do not automatically prepare a dog for hours of climbing and rough ground. Increase the demands gradually, then check how your dog moves later that day and the next morning.

Change one variable at a time: duration, elevation or surface.

Build Start from a comfortable routine walk. Add time first, then gentle climb or rougher ground.
Rehearse Try a shorter route with similar terrain, equipment, rest stops and water planning.
Recover Check gait, energy and paws later and the next morning. Scale back if movement is not normal.

Know when the trail ends

If a pad is cut, stop walking, rinse with clean water and remove only loose, easily reached debris. Apply steady pressure with clean gauze or cloth, then use a loose non-stick dressing for transport.

Get urgent veterinary help for severe bleeding, bleeding that continues after 10 to 15 minutes of steady pressure, a deep or gaping wound, exposed tissue, a deeply embedded object, or if your dog cannot stand or walk.
