Heat and hydration safety

Plan for shade and water, recognise heat stress early, and know how to cool your dog before the trail becomes an emergency.

3 min guideReviewed August 2026
Before setting out

Is today cool enough for this trail?

Dogs cool mainly by panting, while exercise creates heat of its own. Check all four before you leave.

If symptoms appear

Cool first, travel second

A dog’s temperature can keep rising after exercise stops. Begin cooling immediately, then seek veterinary help.

Stop and shadeEnd exercise. Move off hot ground and into shade. Do not walk on to the next rest point.
Wet and ventilatePour cool or cold tap water over the body, avoiding the nose and mouth. Create airflow. Offer water; never force it.
Call and transportCall a veterinarian promptly. Use a cooled, ventilated vehicle and continue cooling during the journey if possible.

Turn back earlier for higher-risk dogs.Be more conservative with flat-faced, overweight, thick-coated, very young, older, unfit or unwell dogs. Never cover an overheating dog with a wet towel: it can trap heat.
