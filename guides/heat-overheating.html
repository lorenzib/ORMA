Heat and hydration safety

Heat illness can develop quickly during exercise—even when the air feels manageable. Use these checks before you leave, then act on changes in your dog rather than waiting for a temperature threshold.

Dogs cool mainly by panting, and exertion generates heat of its own. Shade, carried water and an early turnaround are safety equipment—not optional comforts.

Suspect overheating? Cool first, travel second.

A dog’s temperature can keep rising after exercise stops. Start cooling immediately, then seek veterinary help.

Stop and shadeEnd exercise. Move off hot ground and into shade. Do not walk on to the next rest point.
Wet and ventilatePour cool or cold tap water over the body, avoiding the nose and mouth. Create airflow. Offer water; never force it.
Call and transportCall a veterinarian promptly. Use a cooled, ventilated vehicle and continue cooling during the journey if possible.

Lower your threshold for turning back for flat-faced, overweight, thick-coated, very young, older, unfit or unwell dogs. Never cover an overheating dog with a wet towel: it can trap heat.
