Taking your dog to a rifugio

Lots of huts welcome dogs, but the terrace, the dining room and a bed for the night are three different questions. Ask about the one you need.

2 min guide

In 20 seconds

  • Ring the hut first. “Dog-friendly” on a website isn’t enough.
  • Terrace, inside and overnight are separate questions. Ask about each one you need.
  • Bring water, a bowl and a lead, and have a plan B.
Start here

Call first

‘Dog-friendly’ on a website tells you very little. Ring the hut and say who’s coming: the date, how many dogs and how big.

Until someone says yes

Don’t count on the hut for water, shade, shelter from a storm or a bed.

Three questions

Terrace, inside, overnight

A yes for one isn’t a yes for the others.

Lunch stop

Terrace

Usually the easy one, but still ask.

Ask: Is there shade, and can they give your dog water?
If it rains

Inside

Ask before you walk in. On a busy day the answer can change.

Ask: Which room, and when? Lead only, or a muzzle too?
For the night

Overnight

CAI rules say a dog in the dormitory has to be agreed with the manager when you book.

Get it in writing. Where your dog sleeps, the fee, bedding and house rules.
Booking

Three steps before you go

1

Send one clear message

Give the date, how many dogs and how big, when you’ll arrive and which space you need.

2

Keep the reply

If you booked months ahead, or the details matter, check again nearer the day.

3

Bring what your dog needs

Water, bowl, lead, poo bags and the agreed bedding. Have another route in mind, and check the lift and muzzle rules too.
